Read it, boss. Here's the rundown:

"Adaptive Privacy Budget Allocation in Federated Learning: A Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning Approach" — Chen, Liao, Ma, Chen (Sun Yat-sen University), ICC 2024.

Problem: In differentially-private federated learning, each client adds Gaussian noise to gradients each communication round, spending from a fixed total privacy budget (ρ, via ρ-zCDP). How that budget is split across rounds affects final model accuracy — gradients are large early (tolerate more noise) and shrink later (need less noise). Naive splits waste budget or hurt accuracy. Two challenges make this hard: (1) the noise-level → accuracy relationship is analytically unknown, (2) clients' allocation decisions are coupled through the shared global model.

Approach: Frame it as a Dec-POMDP, one agent per client.

State: (current round t, client's total budget ρ_k)
Action: continuous noise level σ ∈ [σmin, σmax] for that round
Reward: global accuracy delta between consecutive rounds
Trained with DDPG (actor-critic, continuous actions), plus three tricks:

Parameter sharing — all clients share one policy/critic network (faster training).
Value decomposition network (VDN) — sums per-client critic outputs so the single global reward can still be credited to individual clients (decouples the coupling problem).
Action mapping — an affine transform (solved as a small convex QP via KKT) that rescales the raw allocation so the client's full budget is exactly used, instead of leaving some ρ_k unspent.
Results: Tested on MNIST (10 clients, ρk ∈ [2,4], T=20 rounds) and CIFAR-10 (ρk ∈ [20,40], T=25 rounds) against uniform, arithmetic-sequence, and exponential allocation baselines. Their method wins at final accuracy — 91.05% vs. 88.03% (uniform)/90.02%/90.04% on MNIST (~1% gain), and 47.75% vs. ~46.5–47.6% on CIFAR-10 (~0.2% gain) — though it lags the baselines early in training and catches up by the later rounds. Learned behavior matches intuition: noise level decreases over rounds, and clients with larger total budgets use lower noise per round.
